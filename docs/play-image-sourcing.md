## How to add a photograph

1. Confirm the rights holder, photographer and licence **for the specific photo**. Use an image offered for press coverage only under its explicit terms and only if those terms allow the intended recommendation site.
2. Prefer a photo showing cards/tiles/dice *laid out on a table*, not a box render or a generic board game scene. Avoid identifiable people and personal details where practical.
3. Confirm commercial use is allowed, even if the recommendation also carries an Amazon link. Never download Amazon listing images or users' BoardGameGeek photos without permission.
4. Add the \`image\` block with URL, descriptive alt text, creator, source, licence and change notice. The build displays credits next to the image.
5. Keep originals unmodified if licence conditions for derivative work are unclear; prefer \`object-fit: contain\` instead of cropping. Record adaptations and share-alike requirements where relevant.
6. Check load quality, mobile and desktop presentation, accessibility and link destinations before merging.

## Publishers and potential image leads

- **AMIGO:** [Pressematerial terms](https://blog.amigo-spiele.de/presse/pressematerial/) allow press photographs to be used in print and online media under conditions, but we haven't sourced and verified a suitable specific 6 nimmt! gameplay photograph. An explicit press asset could be an aesthetic improvement over the current Wikimedia photo.
- **Flatout Games:** has a [content creators contact route](https://www.flatout.games/contact) suitable for asking permission for a Cascadia gameplay image.
- **Zoch Verlag:** original Heckmeck manufacturer; official images need specific permission or press licence.
- **Tenby:** user-submitted BoardGameGeek gameplay photos are generally not licensed for reuse; request publisher's press kit instead.
- **The Mind:** avoid assumptions about rights to retailer and player photographs.
- **Bärenpark:** request a properly licensed gameplay photograph from Lookout Games, showing the irregular tiles laid out across the park boards. Do not mistake the separate *Bad News Bears* monorail expansion photographs for the base game.
- **OK Play:** Big Potato has strong official images showing colourful tiles in play; request permission for one rather than copying from their shop. Standard game is for 2–4 players. The additional two colours come from the separately sold OK Play Duo/Duel, not an included six-player set; availability was not verified.

The other six games intentionally retain original typographic artwork until a
quality gameplay image with documented reuse rights is selected.
